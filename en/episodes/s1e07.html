Concept 1 / 4 · The authorisation request

The issuer doesn't see what you buy

When you pay, the terminal sends an authorisation request to the card's issuer. It carries the amount and currency, the identity of the merchant and of the terminal, the activity code, the read mode and the EMV data read from the chip, including a cryptogram. The issuer decides from that context: the card's, the terminal's and the merchant's. The receipt and the items are not in it.

On the right, what the issuer receives. On the left, what stays with the merchant.

The cryptogram is calculated by the chip at the moment of payment. It lets the issuer authenticate the card that was used. An intercepted cryptogram cannot be reused: it is tied to the transaction in progress.

The name the customer sees in their banking app comes from the acquirer. An incorrect name, or the company name instead of the shop sign, is fixed at the acquirer.

Remember: the activity code (MCC, Merchant Category Code) is 4 digits, assigned to the merchant by its acquirer at onboarding. 5411 for supermarkets, 5812 for restaurants, 5541 for petrol stations, 7995 for gambling. The issuer uses it to assess risk, more so online than in store. It also weighs on fees: high-risk activities, such as gambling, are charged more, and some acquirers refuse entire sectors.

Concept 2 / 4 · The request's journey

Where the two seconds go

From the beep to the response, an online authorisation takes about two seconds. The request goes through the terminal, the acquirer, the card network and the issuer, then the response travels back the same way.

Operations on the terminal take the longest: it prepares the message and opens a secure connection. They run locally. The path to the acquirer relies on the internet. The round trip to the issuer, card network included, takes on average less than half a second. If the response doesn't arrive, the terminal waits, then cancels the operation (reversal).

Concept 3 / 4 · The response code

A code, not a sentence

The issuer's response fits in two characters: 00 for approved, and about a hundred other values for declines and special cases. The most emblematic:

CodeMessage
05Do not honor
51Insufficient funds
54Expired card
43Stolen card

The tables are not identical from one network or acquirer to another, and some declines come from the terminal itself (expired card, unsupported card).

Remember: code 05 indicates a decline, but does not explain it: it is a generic error code.

Concept 4 / 4 · Strong authentication

The only decline that isn't one

Sometimes the issuer doesn't decline: it asks to identify the cardholder again, under strong authentication (PSD2). The code is 1A at Visa and 65 at Mastercard. Online, the site sends the customer to confirm in their bank's app, then retries the request. In store, the terminal asks for the PIN.

Fraud on online card payments, in € per €100,000 paid. Source: Observatoire de la sécurité des moyens de paiement (OSMP, Banque de France), 2025 annual report.

Remember: strong authentication combines two elements out of three: what you know (the PIN), what you have (the phone, the card), what you are (the fingerprint, the face).
My take
Having the money in your account is not enough: each card has its own limits, and premium cards don't have the same limits as the others. On a large basket, offering to pay in instalments can unblock declines that have nothing to do with the balance.
